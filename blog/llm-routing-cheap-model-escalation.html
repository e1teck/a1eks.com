LLM Routing: Cheap Model First, Escalate When Uncertain

2026-11-09  ·  LLM Agents Routing Cost

Opus-level accuracy at 4x lower cost, and the trick was not a bigger model. It was teaching a cheap one to ask for help.

A recent Hightouch write-up on LLM judges for identity resolution describes exactly this: a small model handles most cases on its own and escalates only the ones it is unsure about to Opus. The result is the accuracy of the expensive model at a cost much closer to the cheap one.

This is the routing pattern from Anthropic's "Building Effective Agents": classify the input, then send it to a specialized handler.

  • Classify each request: easy, medium, hard.
  • Route easy cases to a small model, hard cases to the flagship.
  • Use uncertainty as the escalation trigger, not a keyword rule.

The catch is stated in the same article: classification must be accurate and predictable. If the router mislabels hard cases as easy, you get cheap answers and silent quality loss. A wrong route is worse than an expensive one.

My take on how to start: run everything on the cheap model, log where it hesitates or disagrees with spot checks, then build the escalation path from that real data.

Cheap-first with a known escalation path beats guessing which model your traffic deserves.
